What the contract cannot do

Most of the ways a token is used against the people holding it are visible in its contract before you buy. CPU ships without those functions — not disabled, not renounced later, simply never written.

✓ No mint function

Supply is created once, in the constructor. Nobody can print more and dilute you.

✓ No blacklist

No address can be frozen or blocked from selling. There is no honeypot switch.

✓ No pause

Trading cannot be halted. There is no pause() to pull.

✓ No fees or limits

No tax on transfers, no max wallet, no max transaction — and no setter to add one.

✓ No owner at all

The token has no admin role to renounce, because it never had one.

✓ Rewards kept separate

The reward contract holds no authority over the token. It cannot mint, move or freeze CPU.

How rewards work

Rewards are paid in a separate BEP-20 held by a claim contract. The usual "reflection" design pays the liquidity pool as if it were a holder, taxes every trade, and needs an owner inside the token. This does none of that.

Holder balances are snapshotted

Balances are read from public chain data. The liquidity pool, burn addresses and the treasury are excluded — a pool holds CPU for traders, not as a holder.

A Merkle root is published on-chain

Each holder's share is a leaf in a Merkle tree. Only the root goes on-chain. Anyone can rebuild the tree from the same public data and confirm the root matches.

The epoch is funded in the same transaction

An epoch cannot be opened without the reward tokens arriving with it, and those funds cannot be withdrawn again until the claim deadline passes.

Holders claim their own share

You submit your proof and the contract pays you directly. Payment always goes to the entitled address, never to whoever sent the transaction.
